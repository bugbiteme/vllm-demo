# Clinical Policy: Medical Necessity Criteria for Advanced Imaging

*Fictitious clinical policy for RAG testing purposes only. Policy number MP-RAD-014. Applies to Meridian Health Partners commercial plans.*

## Scope

This policy defines medical necessity criteria for non-emergency MRI, MRA, and CT imaging of the spine, brain, and major joints. It is used by Meridian Utilization Management to adjudicate prior authorization requests (see `prior-authorization-requirements.md`).

## MRI of the Lumbar Spine — Medical Necessity Criteria

MRI of the lumbar spine is considered medically necessary when **at least one** of the following is met:

1. Low back pain persisting greater than 6 weeks despite conservative treatment (physical therapy, NSAIDs, activity modification), **and** documented functional limitation.
2. Red flag symptoms present: progressive neurologic deficit, cauda equina syndrome, suspected spinal infection, or suspected malignancy with spinal involvement.
3. Prior lumbar spine surgery with new or worsening symptoms.
4. Radiculopathy with positive straight-leg-raise test or corresponding dermatomal findings, being considered for surgical or interventional treatment.

MRI of the lumbar spine is **not** considered medically necessary for uncomplicated acute low back pain of less than 6 weeks' duration without red flag symptoms. Requests meeting only this scenario will be denied with a recommendation for conservative management, with reconsideration after 6 weeks if symptoms persist.

## MRI of the Brain — Medical Necessity Criteria

Considered medically necessary for:

- New-onset seizure without a prior imaging workup.
- Focal neurologic deficit of unclear etiology.
- Suspected stroke or transient ischemic attack, when CT is non-diagnostic.
- Chronic headache with new or changed pattern, or headache with red flag features (thunderclap onset, associated neurologic deficit, papilledema).
- Follow-up surveillance for a previously diagnosed intracranial mass, per an established surveillance interval.

Routine imaging for chronic, stable headache without red flag features or a change in symptom pattern is not considered medically necessary.

## MRI of the Knee — Medical Necessity Criteria

Considered medically necessary when conservative treatment (rest, NSAIDs, physical therapy) for a minimum of 4 weeks has failed to resolve symptoms, **and** one of the following is present:

- Mechanical symptoms suggestive of meniscal or ligamentous injury (locking, giving way, joint line tenderness with positive McMurray test).
- Suspected ligamentous instability on physical exam (positive Lachman, anterior/posterior drawer, or varus/valgus stress test).
- Being considered for surgical intervention.

Acute traumatic knee injury with suspected fracture or acute ligamentous rupture (e.g., audible pop, immediate effusion, inability to bear weight) may bypass the 4-week conservative treatment requirement.

## CT vs. MRI Selection

CT is preferred over MRI as the initial study for: acute head trauma, suspected acute intracranial hemorrhage, and suspected renal or ureteral calculi. MRI requests for these indications where CT has not first been attempted, and CT was not contraindicated, are typically denied pending CT results.

## Documentation Required for Review

- Relevant history and physical exam findings supporting the criteria above.
- Documentation of conservative treatment attempted, including duration and modalities.
- Relevant prior imaging results, if available.
- For surgical candidates: documentation that the result would change management.
